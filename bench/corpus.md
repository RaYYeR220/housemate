# Bench corpus

`hermes housemate bench` measures the wake word and the end-to-end latency on the audio below. Only short clips (each under 200 KB) are in this repository. The long negative audio is downloaded by `bench/corpus.py` into `<Hermes home>/plugin-data/housemate/bench/` and checked against pinned SHA-1 sums.

## Positives (the wake word is said)

| Source | Clips | How it was made | Licence / terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tests/fixtures/wake_*.wav` | 10 | "Hey Jarvis." from 4 ElevenLabs voices (roger, sarah, george, jessica; `eleven_flash_v2_5`) and 5 Edge neural voices (en-GB Sonia, en-IN Neerja, en-US Guy, en-IE Connor, en-NZ Molly), plus "Hey Jarvis, what's two plus two?" (en-US Aria). 16 kHz mono. `tests/fixtures/manifest.json` lists each one. | Synthetic speech we generated for testing. |
| Generated by `corpus.make_positives` (not committed) | up to 48 | "Hey Jarvis." and "Hey Jarvis, what's the weather like tomorrow?" from 24 Edge neural voices (US, GB, AU, CA, IN, IE, NZ, ZA, SG, PH English). It needs `edge-tts` and ffmpeg; a voice that fails is skipped. | Synthetic speech, generated on the user's machine. |

Each positive is scored twice: clean (1 s of silence before it), and mixed over public-domain speech from the negative set at 10 dB below the wake phrase (the TV-in-the-background case).

## Near misses (should not wake)

`tests/fixtures/neg_*.wav`: 5 Edge-voice phrases that sound close to the wake word ("Hey there", "Jars of jam", "Hey, Davis!", "Hey, Marvin, are you coming?", "The harvest was early this year").

## Negatives (long speech without the wake word)

LibriVox **Short Story Collection Vol. 062**: 20 short stories read by different volunteers, 9.2 hours in total.

- Source: <https://archive.org/details/shortstorycollection062_1512_librivox> (the 64 kbps MP3s).
- Licence: public domain. LibriVox recordings are dedicated to the public domain, and archive.org marks the item with Public Domain Mark 1.0.
- `bench/corpus.py` pins every file by name, length and archive.org's own SHA-1. ffmpeg decodes the files to 16 kHz mono on the fly.
- False accepts are counted with the listener's own rule. A score at or above the threshold fires once. The detector re-arms only after the score falls below the threshold again, and never within 1 s.

## Latency clips

- `tests/fixtures/wake_jarvis-math_enusaria.wav`: "Hey Jarvis, what's two plus two?" (Edge, en-US Aria).
- `bench/clips/three_times_three_enusguy.wav`: "Hey Jarvis, what is three times three?" (Edge, en-US Guy).

The end of speech in each clip is found with Silero VAD (the last 20 ms frame with speech probability of 0.5 or more). Latency runs from the moment the satellite sends that frame to the moment it receives the first frame of the spoken reply.
